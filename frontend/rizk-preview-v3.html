How RIZK Works

An on-chain catastrophe bond market that connects investors with natural disaster risk

What is a Catastrophe Bond?

A catastrophe bond (catbond) is a financial instrument where investors provide capital to cover potential losses from natural disasters. In return, the entity seeking protection (the "risk holder") pays investors a premium — typically much higher than traditional bond yields — for the duration of the coverage period.

🏛️

Risk Holder

A government fund, insurer, or organization that needs protection against catastrophe losses. They pay a quarterly premium to investors in exchange for coverage. Think of them as buying an insurance policy, funded by the bond market.

💰

Investor

You. You commit capital to the protection pool and receive catbond tokens representing your position. You earn premium yield throughout the coverage period. If no disaster occurs, you get your full capital back at maturity.

📊

Parametric Trigger

Payouts are triggered by objective, measurable parameters — like wind speed for hurricanes or magnitude for earthquakes — not by estimated losses. This means faster, transparent, dispute-free settlement.

🪙

Catbond Tokens

Your position is represented as tradable ERC-20 tokens. You can hold them to earn yield, or sell them on the built-in order book if you want to exit early. Other investors can buy in after the initial commitment phase.

Market Lifecycle

Every protection market goes through four phases. Understanding these helps you know exactly where your capital is and what happens next.

Phase 1
Commitment
Investors pledge capital and post a 1% bond as a deposit guarantee. This ensures committed investors follow through.
Phase 2
Deposit Day
Committed investors deposit their full amount within 24 hours. Priority round first, then open to anyone. Bonds refunded on deposit.
Phase 3
Active Coverage
Capital is locked. Risk holder pays quarterly premium to token holders. Tokens trade freely on the order book. Oracle monitors for trigger events.
Phase 4
Settlement
If no event: full principal + premium returned. If triggered: a portion pays out to the risk holder based on the severity tier. Remaining capital returned.

Trading Catbond Tokens

Once a market is active, your position is fully liquid. Here's how the token economics work.

Why Would Someone Buy a Token?

Catbond tokens represent a claim on the underlying capital plus future premium payments. If you buy a token at $0.95 and no disaster occurs, it redeems at $1.00 at maturity — that's a 5.26% return, plus any premium yield earned while holding.

Token prices fluctuate based on perceived risk. During hurricane season, prices might dip (higher perceived risk = higher potential returns for buyers). After the season passes, prices recover toward $1.00.

Understanding the Risk

Catastrophe bonds carry real risk. If a qualifying event occurs, investors lose a portion of their capital based on the severity tier defined in the coverage agreement. This is the tradeoff for the high premium yield.

🟡

Low Severity

Minor events trigger a partial payout. For hurricanes, a Category 1 might slash 25% of the pool. You'd get back $0.75 per token.

🟠

Medium Severity

Moderate events trigger larger payouts. A Category 3 hurricane might slash 50%. You'd get back $0.50 per token.

🔴

Maximum Severity

Extreme events can trigger 100% payout. A Category 5 hurricane would mean total loss of capital. Tokens redeem at $0.00.
